the Riemann zero gas:\\ amplitude conservation, a thermal Bragg mirror,
and the two-sum interference}

\begin{abstract}
Two companion notes measured how the local statistics of Riemann zeros
respond to the prime oscillations of the explicit formula: an amplitude
channel $w(\tau)$ and a zero-displacement channel $v(\tau)$, functions of
$\tau = \log p / L$, $L = \log\frac{t}{2\pi}$. Here we organize those
measurements into a coherent response theory --- after first identifying,
mechanically explaining, and correcting a \emph{sampling-grid
systematic}: the gap midpoints at which all wave phases are evaluated are
themselves displaced by the prime waves, so regressions on restricted
frequency bases transfer omitted-line content into the measured
coefficients. A ground-truth test on the real grid shows a four-prime
basis biases $w$ downward by $15$--$25\%$, while a complete basis over
all prime powers recovers the truth to $\sim 1\%$; every number below
uses the complete-basis convention. (i) The two channels obey a tight
linear constraint, $\sqrt{w} = 1.017 - 0.884\,v$ (residual RMS $0.0075$;
slope $\pm 0.021$ under window-block bootstrap), which extends unchanged
to prime powers: $u(p^k)$ falls on the same $\tau$-curve as the primes.
The constraint survives an out-of-sample test on unseen primes; among
equal-parameter alternatives intensity-unitarity fails by a factor $6$,
while the $\sqrt{w}$-linear and $w$-linear forms are at present
statistically indistinguishable. (ii) Extending the measurements to
$\tau = 0.61$ shows that $w$ \emph{changes sign} at
$\tau_0 = 0.447 \pm 0.005$, with response phases that jump from $0$ to
$\pi$ without rotating (quadrature below $8\%$ through the crossing):
both channels are real, consistent with a lossless (reactive) medium.
(iii) A thermal Bragg model \emph{reproduces} the crossing: the ideal
mirror sits at the Riemann--Siegel fold $\tau = 1/2$; dressing it with
the measured zero-displacement jitter and the measured beyond-fold
plateau places the zero at $0.443$, within $1\sigma$ of the measurement,
with unit coherent amplitude ($A = 1.02$). (iv) The structure factor
$(1-2\tau)$ is derived in three lines as the pair count of the folded
main sum; pointwise $|Z|^2$ regressions --- dense-grid measurements
immune to the sampling systematic --- instead follow the
shifted-second-moment family $\approx 2(1-\tau)$ through and beyond the
fold, so that the complementary \emph{mirror channel} is a \emph{tent}
peaked at the fold: the approximate functional equation made audible as
an interference channel. (v) Stripping the measured waves leaves a core
correlation $r^* = 0.977$, insensitive to the basis; the finer
decomposition of the residual variance and the standing-wave refinement
of the thermal model are quoted under the previous convention and
flagged for re-measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The first companion note \cite{companion1} measured the joint law of
consecutive-zero gaps and between-zero maxima of Hardy's $Z$-function and
its deviation from CUE characteristic polynomials. The second
\cite{companion2} dissected that deviation into explicit-formula
channels: each prime wave $\cos(t \log p)$ enters the amplitudes at full
weight and splits between a direct amplitude channel $w$ and a
zero-displacement channel $v$, both collapsing onto one-variable laws in
the Berry variable $\tau = \log p / L$ \cite{Berry88}; the channel
decomposition itself is an observable-level counterpart of the hybrid
Euler--Hadamard product \cite{GHK07}. This note asks what \emph{physics} organizes those
curves, and answers with five measured structures: a two-channel linear
constraint, a sign-crossing, a thermal Bragg model that reproduces the
crossing's location, an arithmetic derivation of the structure factor,
and the measurement of the complementary mirror channel. A sixth
structure imposed itself during the final audit: the measurement grid
itself responds to the primes, and Section~\ref{sec:grid} documents the
systematic and the corrected convention now used throughout. Throughout, the style
of the companions is kept: no closed forms are fitted; every ingredient
is either measured or derived; and every claim is accompanied by the
test that tried to kill it.

Data and conventions are those of \cite{companion1, companion2}: eleven
amplitude windows spanning $t = 1.2\times10^5$ to $2.7\times10^{11}$
(the last from Odlyzko's table of zeros near the $10^{12}$-th
\cite{OdlyzkoTables}), plus six low windows down to $t \approx 1.2\times
10^3$ for the extended-$\tau$ measurements; channels are measured by
linear regression of $\log$(unfolded maxima) and $\log$(unfolded gaps)
on $\cos(t_n \log p), \sin(t_n \log p)$ at gap midpoints, with own-gap
controls for $w$. All channel coefficients are measured with
\emph{complete} regression bases (Section~\ref{sec:grid}).

\section{The sampling-grid systematic}\label{sec:grid}

All per-gap channel measurements in this program regress log-maxima and
log-gaps on wave columns evaluated at gap midpoints. The midpoints are
determined by the zeros alone --- exogenous to the amplitudes --- but
they are not a passive grid: the zeros are displaced by the prime waves
(the $v$ channel itself), so the sampling pattern carries the primes. On
the real midpoints a wave column at one frequency acquires a small,
systematic correlation with the columns of \emph{other} frequencies
(Gram $R^2 \approx 7\times10^{-4}$ against the small-prime block for a
near-fold column; identically zero on the smooth Riemann--von Mangoldt
grid). A regression on a restricted basis therefore transfers
omitted-line content into the retained coefficients, and the per-prime
normalization $w = \text{coefficient} \times p^{1/2}$ amplifies the
transfer by $p^{1/2}$, so the bias grows toward the fold.

Two measurements make this quantitative. A basis ladder on the near-fold
band shows the transferred share converging as the basis completes: the
apparent beyond-fold plateau $-0.354$ under a band-only basis shrinks to
$-0.042$ under the full sub-band basis and is stable under further
extension. And a ground-truth test --- synthetic amplitudes with known
line content built on the \emph{real} midpoint grid --- shows a
four-prime basis under-measures $w$ by $15$--$25\%$ ($21\sigma$), while
the complete basis recovers the truth to $\sim 1\%$.

Convention adopted throughout this note and the revised companion: all
channel coefficients are measured with complete bases --- every prime
power $p^k$ with $\log p^k / L \le 0.45$ (to $0.55$ for the
extended-$\tau$ curve), capped at $q \le 720$. The $360 \to 720$ cap
step moves the constraint slope below by $\sim 1\%$, which we quote as
the residual basis-cutoff systematic. Three classes of results are
immune by construction: dense-grid pointwise regressions (the tent of
Section~\ref{sec:tent}), regression-free statistics (the dephasing
scale $s_{\mathrm{eff}}$), and residual correlations (the stripped core
$r^*$, which moves only from $0.9783$ to $0.9774$). The first companion
note contains no channel regressions and is unaffected.

\section{The two-channel constraint}

The second companion's sum rule --- total prime content of unit weight
--- suggested that the two channels share a budget. Under the
complete-basis convention the sharing is a strikingly tight linear
constraint.

\begin{observation}[Two-channel constraint]
Across $44$ matched $(w, v)$ pairs ($4$ primes $\times$ $11$ windows,
$t$ from $10^5$ to $2.7\times10^{11}$), the free fit gives
$\sqrt{w} = 1.017 - 0.884\,v$ with residual RMS $0.0075$ (slope
$\pm 0.021$ under window-block bootstrap), at the per-prime scatter
floor. Constraining the intercept to $1$ worsens the fit (RMS $0.0102$):
the intercept exceeds unity by $\approx 2\%$, comparable to the
residual basis-cutoff systematic, so we do not attach physical
significance to the excess.
\end{observation}

The constraint extends beyond the primes. Measuring the prime-power
lines under the same convention and normalizing by the explicit-formula
weight $p^{-k/2}/k$ gives $u(4) = 0.700 \pm 0.002$,
$u(8) = 0.560 \pm 0.003$, $u(9) = 0.530 \pm 0.002$,
$u(25) = 0.338 \pm 0.004$, $u(27) = 0.335 \pm 0.006$ --- each equal,
within errors, to the prime curve $w(\tau)$ evaluated at that line's own
$\tau = \log p^k / L$. The transmission law is a law of prime powers,
governed by $\tau$ alone.

Stress tests (Figure~\ref{fig:law}). \emph{Out of sample}: $w$ for
$p = 11, 13$ --- not used in the fit, extending the $v$-range ---
follows the free line with RMS $0.0269$ over $22$ pairs, with a
one-sided mean offset of $-0.018$: the constraint survives, though less
crisply than under the previous convention; we suspect genuine curvature
at larger $\tau$. \emph{Form race}: among equal-parameter alternatives
the intensity-unitarity form $w + c v^2 = 1$ fails by a factor $6$ in
RMS; the $\sqrt{w}$-linear and $w$-linear forms, by contrast, are at
present statistically indistinguishable ($0.0127$ vs $0.0099$ in the
$w$-scale), so the data no longer single out amplitude over intensity as
the linearly shared quantity --- an honest retreat from the previous
version of this note.

A cautionary record. Under the previous (restricted-basis) convention
the fit gave $\sqrt{w} = 0.990 - 1.025\,v$, the constrained slope
$1.068$ matched a quadrature phase-bookkeeping ratio $\beta/2 = 1.07$,
and we presented that agreement as a motivated coefficient. The
corrected slope $0.884 \pm 0.021$ does not match it. The bookkeeping
coincidence was an artifact of the sampling-grid bias --- precisely the
kind of trap this project's audit culture exists to catch, and we record
it as such.

\section{The sign crossing and losslessness}

Extending the channel measurements to the primes up to $31$ (eleven
primes) over twelve windows ($132$ points, $\tau$ up to $0.61$, all
complete-basis) shows that the amplitude channel does not merely shrink
--- it \emph{crosses zero} at
\[
\tau_0 \;=\; 0.447 \pm 0.005
\]
(window-block bootstrap over the $\tau \in [0.30, 0.58]$ points) and
settles on a shallow negative plateau: beyond the crossing, peaks are
slightly \emph{low} where the prime wave is high. The plateau depth is
$-0.05$ to $-0.09$ depending on how far the covariate basis is extended
beyond the fold --- much shallower than the $-0.16$ of the previous
convention, most of which the audit exposed as basis transfer. Two
qualifications, sharpened by the audit: the statement ``peaks are low
where the wave is high'' holds \emph{at fixed observed gap} --- without
the own-gap controls the unconditional near-fold response is positive
and of order unity --- and the crossing lies within $1.5\sigma$ of the
coarse exhaustion-analysis crossover $\tau^* = 0.40 \pm 0.03$ of
\cite{companion2}; the identification of $\tau^*$ with the zero of $w$
remains plausible but awaits a complete-basis re-measurement of the
exhaustion curve.

The response phases are diagnostic. Through the crossing the phase of
$w$ jumps from $\approx 0$ to $\approx \pi$ with quadrature-to-in-phase
ratios below $0.011$ for $\tau < 0.35$, $0.08$ through the crossing
region, and $0.14$ beyond; the phase of $v$ stays pinned at $0$. A
damped resonance would rotate through $\pm\pi/2$; a real response that
changes sign is the signature of a \emph{lossless} (conservative,
Hermitian) medium. The curtain does not absorb the prime waves; it
redistributes and reflects them --- consistent, at the level of response
theory, with a Hermitian generator underneath.

\section{The thermal Bragg mirror}

Where should the crossing be? The Riemann--Siegel main sum contains only
$n \le N = \sqrt{t/2\pi}$, i.e.\ $\log n \le L/2$: the amplitude has a
sharp spectral fold at $\tau = 1/2$, and a wave whose period matches two
lattice spacings ($1/\tau = 2$) is precisely the Bragg condition of the
zero lattice. The ideal mirror sits at $\tau = 1/2$; the measured
crossing sits at $0.447$. The shift is thermal.

Model: $w(\tau) = A\,(1 - 2\tau)\, e^{-c \tau^2}\,[\tau < 1/2] + B$,
where $(1-2\tau)$ is the ideal structure factor (derived in
Section~\ref{sec:fold}), $B$ is the indirect channel measured as the
beyond-fold plateau ($B = -0.092$ on the corrected curve), and the
Gaussian factor is Debye--Waller dephasing by the zeros' displacement
jitter. The jitter is \emph{measured}: comparing gap midpoints with the
Riemann--von Mangoldt flow gives $\sigma_u$ per window; in unfolded
units it grows from $0.198$ to $0.272$ across our windows --- the
crystal warms logarithmically, as the classical $S(t)$ variance suggests
--- giving $c = (L \sigma_u)^2/2 = 0.77$--$1.46$, mean $1.17$.

\begin{observation}[The crossing is reproduced]
With $c$ fixed from the measured jitter and $B$ fixed from the measured
plateau, the single remaining parameter fits to $A = 1.02$ (unit
coherent amplitude), and the model's zero lands at $\tau = 0.443$ ---
within $1\sigma$ of the measured $0.447 \pm 0.005$
(Figure~\ref{fig:crossing}). Under the previous convention the model
and the measurement disagreed by more than the thermal family's internal
spread, and the previous version of this note could claim only a bracket
$[0.37, 0.41]$; the sampling-grid correction moved the measured crossing
onto the model. Nothing about the model changed --- only the
measurement.
\end{observation}

Two refinements of the previous version are flagged rather than
asserted: the window-temperature collapse of the fixed-$\tau$ scatter
(to $\pm 0.003$) and the damping-scale race that preferred the
standing-wave factor were both computed on the previous convention's
curve, and await re-measurement on the corrected one. The
model-independent ingredient stands: measured as the ratio of log
characteristic functions of the displacement series, with no Gaussian
assumption and no regression,
\[
s_{\mathrm{eff}} \;=\; \frac{\ln |\langle e^{2 i k u}\rangle|}
{\ln |\langle e^{i k u}\rangle|} \;=\; 4.11 \pm 0.04 ,
\]
the Gaussian value $4$ dressed by the measured sub-Gaussian displacement
statistics (excess kurtosis $-0.3$ to $-0.5$ --- level repulsion
suppressing the tails).

\section{The structure factor, derived}\label{sec:fold}

\begin{derivation}
Write $Z = 2\sum_{n \le N} n^{-1/2} \cos\phi_n$,
$\phi_n = \theta(t) - t \log n$. In $|Z|^2$ the frequency $\log p$ is
produced exactly by the pairs $(m, mp)$ with $mp \le N$, each
contributing weight $4 (m \cdot mp)^{-1/2}$, while the DC part is
$2 \sum_{n \le N} n^{-1}$. Hence the relative modulation is
\[
R_{\mathrm{direct}}(p) \;=\; 2\, p^{-1/2}\,
\frac{S_1(N/p)}{S_1(N)}, \qquad S_1(x) = \sum_{m \le x} \frac{1}{m},
\]
which is $p^{-1/2} \cdot 2(1 - 2\tau)$ in the continuum limit and
vanishes identically for $p > N$.
\end{derivation}

The structure factor $(1-2\tau)$ of the Bragg model is therefore not a
guess: it is the pair-counting arithmetic of the folded sum, with a
sharp horizon at the fold.

\section{The mirror channel: a tent at the fold}\label{sec:tent}

Testing the derivation pointwise --- regressing $|Z(t)|^2$ on the prime
waves over fine grids ($1.3 \times 10^6$ points per window, two windows)
--- produced a surprise: the measured modulation does \emph{not} vanish
at the fold. It follows, through and beyond the fold, the
shifted-second-moment family
\[
R(p) \;\approx\; 2\, p^{-1/2}\,
\frac{L - \log p + c_0}{L + c_0} \;\approx\; p^{-1/2}\cdot 2 (1 - \tau),
\]
dying only at the full horizon $p \approx t/2\pi$
(cf.\ the classical mean-value theorems for
$\int |\zeta|^2 (m/n)^{it}$ \cite{BCHB85}). The difference
\[
R_{\mathrm{mirror}} \;=\; R_{\mathrm{measured}} - R_{\mathrm{direct}}
\]
is a \emph{tent}: it rises as $2\tau$ below the fold, peaks at the fold
with value $1$, and descends as $2(1-\tau)$ beyond
(Figure~\ref{fig:tent}). Its mechanism is the chirp cross-terms
$\cos(2\theta(t) - t \log nm)$ --- precisely the interference between
the two mirror-image sums of the approximate functional equation ---
which lock onto frequency $\log p$ at the stationary-phase products
$nm \approx (t/2\pi)/p$. Beyond the fold, where no direct pairs exist,
everything one hears is the mirrored sum: the functional equation of
$\zeta$, measured as an interference channel. The parameter-free tent
sits systematically below the two-window measurements by $6$--$20\%$;
the exact normalization awaits the true main-term constants (the free fit
prefers $c_0 \approx 1.1$ over the naive $2\gamma - 1$). Measurements
at $\tau \gtrsim 0.62$ are excluded: the zero-spacing comb band sets in
there, and non-prime control frequencies reach the tent scale --- unlike
the max-channel measurements, these pointwise regressions do not yet
carry a full placebo battery, which we flag as a limitation. Being
dense-grid pointwise measurements, they are immune to the sampling-grid
systematic of Section~\ref{sec:grid}.

\section{The residual variance, decomposed}

The companion note's residual amplitude variance
$V_{\mathrm{res}}(L) = 0.163 \to 0.244$ and its decomposition ---
$\sim 4.5\%$ unstripped tail, a gap-modulated interaction share of
$25$--$45\%$, and a core growing at $0.035$ per unit $L$, the CUE rate
at one tenth the amplitude --- were measured under the previous
convention. The headline that survives the audit unchanged is the
correlation statistic: stripping the measured waves from both series
leaves a core pair correlation $r^* = 0.9783$ under the previous basis
and $0.9774$ under the complete basis --- \emph{insensitive} to the
convention, as a correlation of residuals should be. The variance rates
and the interaction share are flagged for re-measurement; we retain the
qualitative statement that a small intrinsic component grows at close to
the random-matrix tempo.

\section{Synthesis and open problems}

The measured response theory of the zero gas now reads: each prime-power
wave enters at full explicit-formula weight and splits between
transmission and zero displacement along a tight linear constraint
($\sqrt{w} = 1.017 - 0.884\,v$; the $\sqrt{w}$-versus-$w$ form question
is open); the medium is consistent with lossless, with a Bragg mirror at
the Riemann--Siegel fold whose observed position $0.447$ is reproduced
by dressing the ideal mirror with the measured thermal jitter; the ideal
structure factor is the fold's pair-counting arithmetic; beyond the fold
the response is carried by the functional equation's mirror sum, whose
interference channel is a tent peaked at the fold; the stripped core
correlation $0.977$ is basis-insensitive; and the measurement grid
itself is part of the medium --- the zeros' displacement field couples
every restricted regression to the omitted lines.

Open problems: (1) the exact tent normalization from the classical
shifted-moment main terms, including the $\chi$-side; (2) the mapping
of the pointwise mirror channel to the corrected max-channel plateau
($-0.05$ to $-0.09$): sign and size; (3) partial-reflection dressing
$r(\tau)$ below the Bragg condition (a first $2\theta$-locked candidate
at the few-percent level is recorded in the project log); (4) a
derivation of the constraint's slope $0.884$ and intercept $1.017$;
(5) the distribution of the intrinsic core --- is it GUE?; (6)
universality: does the same response theory hold for Dirichlet
$L$-functions?; (7) a mechanistic theory of the sampling-grid coupling
--- the grid the zeros provide is not a passive ruler.
