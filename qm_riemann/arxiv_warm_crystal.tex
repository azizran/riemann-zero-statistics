\begin{abstract}
Three companion notes measured how the local statistics of Riemann zeros
respond to the prime waves of the explicit formula, and identified a
sampling-grid systematic: the gap midpoints on which all such
measurements stand are themselves displaced by the primes. Here we turn
the grid into the object. Its structure factor
$\hat G(\omega) = \langle e^{i\omega t_n}\rangle$ --- the diffraction
pattern of the zero lattice --- decomposes into three measured
components: \emph{arithmetic spots} at $\omega = \log p^k$,
\emph{Bragg combs} damped by the lattice temperature, and a
\emph{dark field} --- a property of the midpoint sampling grid, not of
the zeros, which are dark down to the data floor --- lying a factor
$15$--$490$ below shot noise once window leakage is removed
(hyperuniformity seen directly in diffraction). The spots obey an
absolute, parameter-free law derived from the explicit formula,
$|\hat G(\log q)| = \Lambda(q)\,(L\sqrt{q})^{-1}\cos(\pi\tau)
\cdot \mathrm{DW}$, verified to $0.1$--$4\%$ across fourteen prime and
prime-power lines, with phases consistent with pure real (deviations $\le 0.65^\circ$); the comb
brightness tracks the measured window temperature ($e^{-c}$, ratio
$0.885$ constant across windows, most of the deviation from Gaussian
explained by the measured negative excess kurtosis); and binned over
the spots, the pattern rebuilds the Montgomery ramp in the first
line-bearing bin --- taper-invariant, $99.9\%$ of it carried by the
lines themselves, and predicted to $2\%$ by the absolute law with no
free parameter --- Berry's semiclassical decomposition observed
directly. The grid systematic of the companion revisions closes into an
identity: all Gram couplings between wave columns are values of
$\hat G$, and the regression-dressing lives in the off-line continuum
of the midpoint grid --- the non-linear half-gap sampling sidebands of
the arithmetic lines, whose level a noiseless explicit-formula lattice
reproduces to $6\%$. On the gap side, the
same displacement field yields a derived sum rule for the
zero-displacement channel --- onset $v = 2\tau$ and saturation scale
$2/\pi$ --- with $\Lambda$-weighting confirmed for prime powers
($k \cdot v(p^k)$ lands on the prime curve to $1$--$5\%$); the residual
is a \emph{screening function} $D(\tau)$, isolated by injection
calibration. Finally, the conditional gap response splits into two
branches: to its own spontaneous fluctuations the gas responds like CUE for
$\tau \gtrsim 0.3$ (within $3\%$, reaching $0.3\%$ at the Nyquist
wavevector --- an agreement test not reducible to pair-correlation
statistics), while below the first prime line it is effectively frozen
($D \approx 0.04$); the coherent prime branch instead starts near unity
and is screened to $0.48$, the two branches crossing at
$\tau^* \approx 0.14$. Two candidate mechanisms for the measured prime-power weight deficit
are refuted --- one by a phase-purity test, one quantitatively --- and
the surviving constraints are stated as open problems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The first three notes of this program \cite{companion1, companion2,
companion3} measured the joint law of consecutive-zero gaps $\delta_n$
and between-zero maxima of Hardy's $Z$, dissected it into explicit-formula
channels --- an amplitude transmission $w(\tau)$ and a zero-displacement
response $v(\tau)$, $\tau = \log p / L$, $L = \log\frac{t}{2\pi}$ ---
and organized the channels into a response theory: a two-channel linear
constraint, a sign crossing at $\tau_0 = 0.447$, a thermal Bragg mirror
at the Riemann--Siegel fold that reproduces it, and a mirror
(functional-equation) interference channel.

The revision of those notes was forced by a discovery about the
\emph{measurement} itself: every per-gap regression stands on the gap
midpoints, and the midpoints are not a passive grid --- the zeros are
displaced by the prime waves, so the grid carries the primes, restricted
regression bases transfer omitted-line content into retained
coefficients, and a ground-truth test showed the transfer reaches
$0.14$--$0.24$ in absolute $w$ units. The companions correct for this by
the complete-basis convention. The present note takes the opposite
attitude: \emph{the bending ruler is not a nuisance --- it is the
cleanest window into the zero lattice that this program has found.}
Throughout, the style of the companions is kept: no closed forms are
fitted; every ingredient is measured or derived; every claim is
accompanied by the test that tried to kill it, and two failed
derivations are reported as such.

Data: the six primary amplitude windows of \cite{companion2}
($4\times10^4$ gaps each, $L = 9.86$ to $12.45$), with the auxiliary
windows of the companions where noted. All measurements are per-window
and pooled; placebo and surrogate controls accompany each new
observable.

\section{The diffraction pattern}\label{sec:diff}

Define the structure factor of the midpoint grid,
\[
\hat G(\omega) \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{n} e^{i\omega t_n},
\qquad t_n = \text{gap midpoints},
\]
--- operationally, the diffraction pattern of the zero lattice. Three
components appear (Figure~\ref{fig:diff}).

\emph{Arithmetic spots.} At $\omega = \log q$, $q = p^k$, the pattern
shows sharp spots. To first order in the displacement field $u$
(defined by $t_n = t_n^{\mathrm{smooth}} + u_n$),
$\hat G(\omega) \approx i\omega \langle e^{i\omega t^{s}} u\rangle$, so
a $u$-line of amplitude $U_q$ at $\log q$ produces a spot
$|\hat G| = \tfrac{\omega}{2} U_q \cos(\pi\tau)$, the $\cos(\pi\tau)$
being the midpoint-averaging factor over one lattice spacing
(measured independently by injection, below). The spots are
\emph{phase-locked}: across eleven primes, six windows, and eight prime
powers, $\arg \hat G(\log q) = 180.0^\circ$--$180.6^\circ$,
deviations well inside the statistical phase uncertainties ($3$--$5^\circ$
on the strong spots, up to $\pm 24^\circ$ on the weakest), with
quadrature parts consistent with zero at the $10^{-4}$ level --- the
displacement field is sine-locked, exactly as a real gap-channel
response demands.

\emph{Bragg comb.} In the unfolded frame the lattice's own reflection
sits at $\omega_x = 2\pi k$. Its first-order brightness is the
characteristic function of the midpoint phase; the Gaussian
(Debye--Waller) estimate $e^{-c}$, with $c = (2\pi\sigma_u)^2/2$ the
measured jitter of the companion notes, overshoots by a constant factor:
measured/Gaussian $= 0.88$--$0.89$ across all six windows. The measured
excess kurtosis of the wrapped midpoint phase ($-0.75$ to $-0.79$)
explains most of the gap through the Edgeworth correction
($0.32 \to 0.27$ vs measured $0.256$ at $L = 9.86$; the $0.885$ ratio
is quoted against the unfolded-jitter Gaussian $e^{-c}$, the Edgeworth
chain against the wrapped-phase Gaussian --- two baselines differing by
convention); a $\sim 6\%$
remainder is left to higher cumulants. The second-order comb is
extinguished (measured at the noise floor), as strong dephasing
predicts. The comb brightness thus tracks the window temperature
window-by-window --- a third, independent thermometer for the thermal
Bragg model of \cite{companion3}.

\emph{Dark field.} Off the lines and away from the comb, the pattern is
\emph{dark} --- but how dark is a statement about the window as much as
about the lattice, and the two have to be separated. With a rectangular
window the median $|\hat G|$ over the $956$ off-line frequencies of the
scan (of $1200$) is $1.5\times10^{-4}$, a factor $33$ \emph{below} the
shot-noise level $1/\sqrt{n}$ and below a shuffled-gap surrogate (which
destroys the anticorrelations and raises the floor to
$1.4\times10^{-3}$). A perfect lattice pushed through the identical
estimator, however, returns $1.20$ times that floor for $\omega < 1$:
there the rectangular measurement is spectral leakage and not zeros
(over the full band, and above $\omega = 3$, it stands $28$ and $293$
times clear of leakage). Under a Hann taper the leakage collapses by
twenty orders of magnitude while the floor survives --- it then exceeds
the windowed perfect lattice by $4.8\times10^{11}$ ($\omega < 1$) to
$3.1\times10^{19}$ ($\omega > 3$), so it is measured content, not
instrument --- and the darkness statement sharpens: the off-line median
lies $48$, $490$ and $15$ times below shot noise over the full band,
below $\omega = 1$, and above $\omega = 3$ respectively. (Levels are
quoted either as $|\hat G|$ or as the taper-invariant intensity
$I(\omega) = |\sum_n w_n e^{i\omega t_n}|^2 / \sum_n w_n^2$, which
equals $n|\hat G|^2$ for a rectangular window and unity for a Poisson
process. All floors are medians over the scan; $I$ is exponentially
distributed off the lines, so the corresponding means are larger by
$1/\ln 2$.)

The honest measure of hyperuniformity is not a comparison with shot
noise but with the field an \emph{independent} gap sequence would
radiate through the same sampling, $1 - |\langle e^{i\omega
g/2}\rangle|^2$: the measured diffuse channel sits $40$ to $1150$ times
below that proxy, and shuffling the gaps --- which destroys their
anticorrelation and nothing else --- raises it $63$-fold, back onto it.
Hyperuniformity of the zero gas --- number-variance rigidity --- is
here seen directly as darkness in the diffraction pattern of the
midpoint grid. All arithmetic content is concentrated in the discrete
spots.

\emph{Whose darkness?} The dark field belongs to the grid, not to the
zeros. Writing the midpoints as $t_n = z_n + g_n/2$ gives the identity
\[
\hat G_{\mathrm{mid}}(\omega) \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{n}
e^{i\omega z_n}\, e^{i\omega g_n/2},
\]
so the midpoints sample the zero lattice through a \emph{fluctuating}
half-gap shift: $\langle e^{i\omega g/2}\rangle$ is a Debye--Waller
factor of the gap distribution, and the fluctuation of that shift is
diffuse scattering. Run on the zeros $z_n$ themselves, the same
estimator returns an off-line floor of $7\times10^{-16}$ in $I$ ---
eight to ten orders below the midpoint grid at the same frequencies,
and itself data-limited, since injecting the $\sigma = 3.3\times10^{-8}$
quantization of our zero finder raises it by $1.3$--$1.5$ times. The
zero-lattice floors quoted here and below are therefore upper bounds,
and the darkness ratios built on them lower bounds.
The interpolation $t_n(c) = z_n + \bar g/2 + c\,(g_n - \bar g)/2$ turns
the mechanism into a curve: $I = 2.4\times10^{-14}$ at $c = 0$, where
the grid is the zero lattice rigidly shifted; $2.3\times10^{-4}$ at
$c = 1/2$; a maximum of $4.2\times10^{-4}$ at $c = 1$, the midpoints;
and back to $2.4\times10^{-14}$ at $c = 2$, where the grid is the zero
lattice again, shifted the other way. The dark field is a
\emph{sampling-phase} effect oscillating between two identical and ten
orders darker lattices, and the midpoint convention of this program
sits exactly on its maximum.

\section{The absolute spot law}\label{sec:abs}

\begin{derivation}
The truncated explicit formula gives the argument function the line
content $S(t) \supset -\tfrac{1}{\pi}\,
\frac{\Lambda(q)}{\sqrt{q}\,\log q}\,\sin(t\log q)$, and the zero
displacement is $u = -S/\bar\rho$ with $\bar\rho = L/2\pi$; hence the
$u$-line amplitude
\[
U_q \;=\; \frac{2\Lambda(q)}{L\sqrt{q}\,\log q},
\]
and the spot law, with no reference to any channel measurement,
\[
|\hat G(\log q)| \;=\; \frac{\Lambda(q)}{L\sqrt{q}}\,
\cos(\pi\tau)\; e^{-\omega^2\sigma_t^2/2}.
\]
\end{derivation}

\begin{observation}[Absolute spot law]
Across the six windows, measured/predicted $= 0.999$ ($p=2$), $1.002$
($p=3$), $1.007$, $1.012$, $1.021$, $1.024$, $1.030$, $1.032$, $1.036$,
$1.039$, $1.040$ ($p=31$); mean $1.022 \pm 0.014$. The prediction has
\emph{no} free parameters and \emph{no} channel input: the explicit
formula predicts the diffraction pattern of the zero lattice at the
percent level. The mild $+2$--$4\%$ rise with $\omega$ is consistent
with the Gaussian DW factor slightly over-damping a sub-Gaussian field
and is left as a second-order refinement.
\end{observation}

For prime powers the same law carries $\Lambda(p^k) = \log p$: the
$1/k$ suppression relative to a $\log q$ weighting. Both channels
confirm it (Section~\ref{sec:gap}); the strong power spots ($q = 4, 8,
9$) obey the absolute law to $1$--$4\%$, while the weak ones fall
progressively below it --- the \emph{power deficit} of
Section~\ref{sec:deficit}.

The spot brightness also closes a loop with the gap channel: the ratio
of the measured spot to the naive $v$-based prediction is a pure
sampling-geometry factor, $\pi\tau\cot(\pi\tau)$ (the gap regression
measures the wave's \emph{difference}, $2\sin(\pi\tau)$, the midpoints
carry its \emph{average}, $\cos(\pi\tau)$), times a constant: measured
ratio$/f(\tau) = 1.083 \pm 0.007$, flat across all eleven primes
(Figure~\ref{fig:geom}). The constant agrees with the inverse of the small-$\tau$ screening of
Section~\ref{sec:gap} to $\sim 1.5\%$ ($1/D$ from the first bin gives
$1.101 \pm 0.003$ against $k_0 = 1.083 \pm 0.007$); the full closure
algebra across $\tau$ --- where $k_0$ stays flat while $D$ falls ---
involves the DW and pipeline factors and is folded into Open
Problem~3. Note $f(1/2) = 0$: the vanishing of midpoint
spots at the fold is the sampling Nyquist condition.

\section{The ruler identity and the origin of the dressing}

The Gram coupling between any two wave columns on the real grid is an
exact functional of $\hat G$:
\[
\langle \cos(\omega_a t)\cos(\omega_b t)\rangle
= \tfrac12\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[\hat G(\omega_a - \omega_b)
+ \hat G(\omega_a + \omega_b)\right],
\]
(and the analogous sine combinations), verified numerically to
$5\times10^{-8}$ against directly computed Grams. The omitted-variable
transfer that biased restricted-basis channel measurements
\cite{companion2, companion3} is therefore entirely a property of the
diffraction pattern. Decomposing the transfer line by line shows it is
\emph{collective} --- all eleven omitted small-prime lines contribute
with the same sign, none dominating --- and the carrier frequencies sit
not on arithmetic lines but in the off-line continuum between them.
Checked against the complete prime-power list ($7327$ lines with
$\log q \le 11.2$; the earlier check used a truncated one), the nearest
line to a carrier is $7$ to $199$ resolution widths away
($2\pi/T = 2.6\times10^{-4}$), even though the surrounding forest at
$\omega \approx 6.5$ has a spacing of only $\Delta\log p \approx 0.01$.
The carriers are real: they survive tapering almost unchanged (the
tapered/untapered $|\hat G|^2$ ratio is $0.62$--$0.67$) and stand
$3$--$7$ times (median $4$--$5$) above the tapered, line-excluded local
floor, which is itself nine orders above the leakage of a smooth grid
put through the same scan.

It is tempting to read this rising off-line field as the thermal
diffuse scattering of the zero crystal. It is not. At the very same
frequencies the spectrum of the zeros
$z_n$ is a further factor $\sim 10^{4}$ darker in $|\hat G|$; what
carries the dressing is the diffuse field that the midpoint sampling
\emph{manufactures} out of the arithmetic lines --- the non-linear
sidebands of the fluctuating half-gap shift. A noiseless lattice built
from the explicit formula alone, with no $\zeta$ data and at the
measured window temperature ($\sigma_{\mathrm{lab}}/\sigma =
1.00$--$1.22$), reproduces the carrier level to $6\%$ in the median,
though only to a factor $0.5$--$5$ frequency by frequency: the
identification is of mechanism and magnitude, not of fine structure.
In one sentence: \emph{the bending of the regressions is arithmetic in
origin, and lives in the diffuse scattering of the sampling grid.}

Binned over frequency, the pattern makes contact with the Montgomery
form factor \cite{Montgomery73}. The form factor is the taper-invariant
intensity $I$ of Section~\ref{sec:diff}, whose bin averages
$\bar F$ sit on the ramp $\alpha = \omega/L$. Below the first line --- below $\log 2$, where the explicit formula has
no atom at all --- the binned average is
$\bar F = 5.6\times10^{-6}$ against the ramp value $0.036$: at least
$6.5\times10^{3}$ times dark on the midpoint grid, at least
$2\times10^{12}$ on the zeros, both instrument-limited bounds. (The
rectangular-window version of this ratio, a factor $18$, is entirely
instrument: an arithmetic-free smooth control grid returns the same
$17.2$.) The first line-bearing bin, by contrast, recovers the ramp
from the spots alone, and does so robustly: $\bar F = 0.1013$ against
the ramp $0.0892$, taper-invariant to $0.2\%$, with $99.91\%$ of the
bin average carried by the on-line points, and predicted by the
absolute spot law of Section~\ref{sec:abs} --- no free parameter, no
channel input --- as $0.0997$, a ratio of $1.016$; on the zero grid the
same bin gives $0.1127$ measured against $0.1086$ predicted, $1.038$.
The off-line continuum inside that bin lies a factor $9.2\times10^{2}$
below the ramp on the midpoints and $2.5\times10^{5}$ on the zeros; a
first rectangular-window pass gave only $10$--$20$ here, the sinc tails
of the two main lobes leaking through too narrow a mask and inflating
the level some ninetyfold. This is
Berry's semiclassical construction of the GUE ramp out of prime lines
\cite{Berry88}, observed directly and now held to a parameter-free
amplitude. At larger $\alpha$ it is the \emph{zero} grid that tracks
the ramp, to $\pm 25\%$ for $\alpha \gtrsim 0.2$, while the midpoint
binning falls behind in the same region where the $v$-channel
saturates: that shortfall is the $\cos(\pi\tau)$ and Debye--Waller
damping of the midpoint sampling, not a property of the zeros. We
record, as a theory target, that the ramp theorem plus the spot law
constitute a sum rule constraining $v(\tau)$ from first principles.

\section{The gap-channel sum rule and the screening function}
\label{sec:gap}

Carrying the derived $U_q$ through the difference geometry of the gap
channel gives a closed-form prediction,
\[
v(\tau) \;=\; \frac{2}{\pi}\,\sin(\pi\tau)\, ,
\]
which is the \emph{rigid} (unscreened) prediction. Its two constants
organize the data once the screening below is included: the measured
complete-basis onset is $(1.7$--$1.8)\,\tau = 2\tau\,D(0)$, and the
measured near-fold level is $\approx 0.47 = (2/\pi)\,
D(\mathrm{fold})$. (The previous convention's onset $2.014\,\tau$
matched the rigid $2\tau$ only through an approximate cancellation of
two systematics --- basis-transfer inflation against screening --- and
is recorded as a coincidence.) Against the $132$ complete-basis
measurements the $\sin(\pi\tau)$ shape holds with free amplitude
$0.530$, with a systematic $\tau$-drift of the pointwise ratio
($0.92 \to 0.74$) that is precisely the screening function below
(Figure~\ref{fig:bridge}).

Injection calibration --- adding a synthetic wave of known amplitude to
the \emph{real} zero sequence and passing it through the identical
pipeline --- shows the cosine (midpoint) geometry accurate to $1$--$9\%$ across
$\tau = 0.10$--$0.40$, linear in the injected amplitude (a fivefold
amplitude change moves the calibration factors by $< 0.7\%$), and the
pipeline nearly faithful on the sine side
($a_v = 1.00$--$0.85$ from $\tau = 0.06$ to $0.50$). The residual,
\[
D(\tau) \;=\; \frac{v_{\mathrm{meas}}}{2\tau\, a_v(\tau)}
\;=\; 0.91 \to 0.48 \quad (\tau: 0.05 \to 0.55),
\]
is therefore a physical \emph{screening function}: the gap (difference)
channel responds to the coherent prime drive less than rigid
displacement predicts, while the position (average) channel responds in
full. An additive core --- fluctuations independent of the lines ---
would give $D \equiv 1$; the measurement excludes it. The zeros move
partly \emph{collectively} under the prime waves: translations are
free, compressions are resisted.

\emph{$\Lambda$-weighting and $1/k$.} For prime powers the derived
$v(p^k) = \tfrac{2}{\pi}\sin(\pi\tau)\,D/k$ predicts that
$k\,v(p^k)$ lands on the prime curve. Measured (ratio test,
independent of $D$'s shape): $1.013 \pm 0.009$ ($q=4$),
$1.005 \pm 0.014$ ($8$), $1.005 \pm 0.009$ ($9$), $0.953 \pm 0.021$
($16$), $0.984 \pm 0.012$ ($25$), $0.953 \pm 0.019$ ($27$); the
alternative ($\log q$ weighting, ratio $\approx k$) is excluded. The
$v$-law is a law of $\Lambda$.

\section{The two-branch response}\label{sec:branches}

Is the screening a property of the gas or of the drive? Define the
conditional gap response at wavevector $\kappa = 2\pi\tau$: the
regression of gap-content on position-content, normalized so that a
rigid displacement wave gives $1$. Three measurements answer
(Figure~\ref{fig:branch}).

\emph{The CUE reference.} For the circular unitary ensemble the
response is computed as a conditional (regression) functional --- the
covariance of gap content on position content over $3000$ free samples;
no perturbed simulation is required, and equality with a potential
response is not assumed --- with the
Diaconis--Shahshahani moments \cite{DS94}
($\langle|\mathrm{Tr}\,U^m|^2\rangle = \min(m, N)$) reproduced as an
internal check. CUE does \emph{not} screen: $D_{\mathrm{CUE}} = 1.00 \to
1.18$ from $\kappa \to 0$ to the Nyquist point.

\emph{The spontaneous branch.} The same conditional response measured
on the zeta grid's \emph{own} off-line fluctuation modes (pseudo-ensembles
of $630$--$950$ line-free frequencies per band; local unfolding, cubic
detrending, and phase-scrambled surrogate floors all verified) gives:
\[
D_{\mathrm{spont}} = 0.04\;(\tau{=}0.04),\; 0.24\;(0.06),\;
0.49\;(0.08),\; 0.66\;(0.10),\; 0.93\;(0.15),\; 0.96\;(0.20),
\]
(each point mixes a band of width $\pm 0.02L$; band-mixing biases the
frozen values \emph{upward}, so the freezing is if anything deeper,
with a band systematic of order $10\%$ on individual points; the
first-line threshold spans $\tau_2 = 0.056$--$0.070$ across windows)
then $1.05$, $1.11$, $1.18$ at $\tau = 0.3$, $0.4$, $0.5$ --- within
$3\%$ of CUE, reaching $0.34\%$ at the Nyquist point (the
$\tau = 0.3$--$0.4$ deviations are same-signed, below CUE; window-scatter
error bars are pending). Two facts stand out. First, for
$\tau \gtrsim 0.3$ the zero gas responds to its own noise like CUE at
the few-percent level: an agreement test that, involving
consecutive-gap contents rather than two-point counts alone, is not
reducible to the pair-correlation function. (One caution on its
reading: a noiseless explicit-formula lattice reproduces the
spontaneous curve to $2$--$6\%$ for $\tau \geq 0.15$ with no CUE input
--- script 106 --- so the agreement is best read as a property of the
conditional sampling estimator on a rigid arithmetic lattice, not as
independent evidence of thermality. The freeze below the first prime
line and the screening of the prime branch are unaffected: the same
lattice fails to produce the screening, leaving it a genuine
many-body response.) Second, below the first prime line
($\tau_2 = \log 2/L \approx 0.067$) the gas is \emph{frozen}: position
modulations exist but gaps do not breathe --- the sub-ramp
(quasi-random) regime of the form factor, where the dark field lies at
least three orders below the GUE ramp on the midpoint grid and at least
twelve on the zeros, is rigid, and the thaw to CUE behavior
occurs across precisely the band where the first prime lines turn on.

\emph{The prime branch.} The coherent arithmetic drive behaves
oppositely: $D_{\mathrm{prime}}$ \emph{starts} near unity at long
wavelengths (where the spontaneous branch is frozen) and is screened to
$0.48$ at the fold (where the spontaneous branch exceeds unity). The
two branches cross at $\tau^* = 0.140$ (range scan $0.13$--$0.15$; the
two branches are measured by different estimators --- log-gap
regression versus direct Fourier covariance --- and the
cross-estimator systematic is not yet budgeted). The prime waves act as
quasi-static deformations of the equilibrium frame --- followed
adiabatically at long wavelength, resisted at short --- while the
spontaneous long-wave modes are collective translations that carry no
compression. A theory of either branch, and of their crossing scale, is
open.

\section{The power deficit: two failed mechanisms and a constraint
set}\label{sec:deficit}

Prime-power lines carry slightly less than full $\Lambda$ weight, a
deficit growing with $q$ and $k$, seen consistently in three
independent objects (unconditioned channel contents, diffraction spots,
and mildly in the gap-ratio test): spot ratios $0.99$ ($q{=}4$), $0.96$
($8$), $0.98$ ($9$), then $0.87$, $0.89$, $0.81$, $0.65$, $0.72$ for
$q = 16, 25, 27, 32, 49$.

Two mechanisms were built and killed. A noiseless laboratory lattice
obeying the implicit zero condition $\bar\rho\,u = -S(t+u)$ with exact
explicit-formula lines reproduces a power-selective deficit of the
right ordering --- but necessarily rotates the power-spot phases by
$10$--$30^\circ$; the real spots sit at $180^\circ$ within $0.01$--$0.65^\circ$ --- on
the strong spots ($q = 4, 8, 9$; statistical uncertainty
$3$--$5^\circ$) this comfortably excludes the predicted
$11$--$18^\circ$ rotations --- so the coherent second-order mechanism
is excluded
(the real field's random component decoheres it). A Bessel-type
harmonic-sampling mechanism (power spots fed by the parent prime line
through the sampling exponential) preserves phase but fails
quantitatively ($J_4$ far too small for $q=16$; the $q=49$ vs $q=25$
ordering inverted relative to the parent-line hierarchy).

What survives is a sharp constraint set for the true mechanism: it is
\emph{phase-preserving} (a real multiplicative factor), \emph{selects
prime powers} while leaving primes exact at the $10^{-3}$ level, and
\emph{grows with both $q$ and $k$}. The natural remaining candidate ---
second-order corrections to the $S(t)$ line amplitudes themselves ---
is stated as an open analytic problem.

\section{Synthesis and open problems}

The zero lattice, viewed through its own diffraction pattern, is a warm
one-dimensional crystal: arithmetic spots whose brightnesses the
explicit formula predicts absolutely; a Bragg comb whose brightness is
the window thermometer; a diffuse scattering of the sampling grid,
arithmetic in origin, that is precisely the agent bending every
restricted regression; and a hyperuniform dark field, two to three
orders below shot noise once window leakage is removed, whose binned
average rebuilds the Montgomery ramp out of prime lines. Two spectra
are in play here, and keeping them apart is the note's main lesson:
the zero spectrum is atomic --- a resolved forest of prime-power lines
separated by true voids --- while the midpoint spectrum is that same
forest plus an arithmetic continuum manufactured by the sampling.
On this substrate the gap channel obeys a derived sum rule
($2\tau$ onset, $2/\pi$ scale) filtered through a measured screening
function, and the conditional response splits into two branches ---
frozen-to-CUE for the gas's own noise, near-unity-to-screened for the
coherent primes --- crossing at $\tau^* \approx 0.14$.

Open problems: (1) the power-deficit mechanism under the three
constraints of Section~\ref{sec:deficit}; (2) a theory of the screening
function $D(\tau)$ and of the spontaneous branch's freeze-to-CUE
transition, including the crossing scale $\tau^* \approx 0.14$; (3) the
ramp-plus-spot-law sum rule as a first-principles derivation of the
$v$-normalization and of $k_0$; (4) the $6\%$ non-Edgeworth remainder
of the comb and the sub-Gaussian refinement of the spot DW factor;
(5) the low-$\tau$ frozen regime as a compressibility statement:
below the first prime line the form factor sits at least
$6.5\times10^{3}$ times below the ramp on the midpoint grid and at
least $2\times10^{12}$ on the zeros --- both instrument-limited bounds
--- while the conditional gap response there is $D \approx 0.04$;
what, precisely, is rigid below the first prime line?; (6) the same
diffraction program for Dirichlet $L$-functions: which spots move,
which constants persist?
